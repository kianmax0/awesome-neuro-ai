The learning guide now lives in [Pathways through NeuroAI](../start-here/pathways.md), with separate routes for different backgrounds and goals. Exercises live in [Projects](../projects/README.md). The former stage headings remain here for existing bookmarks.

## 1. Build the foundations

Use the [student route](../start-here/pathways.md#for-students) and [Learn](../learn/README.md) to fill relevant gaps in biology, programming, or mathematics. The [overview](../start-here/README.md) also provides a no-code entry point.

## 2. Connect the fields

Choose the [machine-learning-to-neuroscience route](../start-here/pathways.md#machine-learning-to-neuroscience) or [neuroscience-to-machine-learning route](../start-here/pathways.md#neuroscience-to-machine-learning).

## 3. Reproduce and compare

Choose a [representation comparison](../projects/README.md#compare-model-representations), [cognitive task comparison](../projects/README.md#compare-models-on-a-cognitive-task), or [spiking-mechanism exercise](../projects/README.md#inspect-a-spiking-mechanism). Each project states its inputs, steps, outputs, and interpretation limits.

## 4. Formulate and test a research question

Follow the [researcher route](../start-here/pathways.md#for-researchers) and write a [research comparison brief](../projects/README.md#write-a-research-comparison-brief).

## How to use these routes

Choose by your existing background and current goal; the routes are editorial suggestions rather than fixed prerequisites. Resource descriptions and access notes remain on their canonical pages. The [teaching route](../start-here/pathways.md#for-teaching) supports courses and reading groups.
